# References and Conceptual Neighbors

This is a starting bibliography, not a claim that existing research already defines “existential euphoria” as used in this project.

## Awe

- Keltner, D., & Haidt, J. (2003). **Approaching awe, a moral, spiritual, and aesthetic emotion.** *Cognition and Emotion, 17*(2), 297–314. https://doi.org/10.1080/02699930302297
  - Important here for the proposal that awe centrally involves **perceived vastness** and a **need for accommodation**.

## Self-transcendent experience

- Yaden, D. B., Haidt, J., Hood, R. W., Jr., Vago, D. R., & Newberg, A. B. (2017). **The varieties of self-transcendent experience.** *Review of General Psychology, 21*(2), 143–160. https://doi.org/10.1037/gpr0000102
  - Useful for distinguishing awe, flow, mindfulness, mystical-type experience, and other states involving decreased self-salience and increased connectedness.

## Philosophical / historical neighbors to investigate

- the sublime (including Burke and Kant)
- existentialism and absurdism
- the “oceanic feeling” associated with Romain Rolland and later discussed by Freud
- cosmicism and cosmic perspective
- peak experience
- wonder and astonishment
- mono no aware
- saudade
- sehnsucht
- religious and nonreligious mystical experience
- aesthetic chills / frisson

These should be studied on their own terms before drawing equivalences. Similarity is not identity.

## Phrase provenance

The exact phrase **“existential euphoria”** has appeared independently before this repository. Examples found during initial exploration include music titles/descriptions and informal first-person descriptions of an “opposite” or counterpart to existential crisis.

That makes provenance a research task rather than an authorship claim. Add documented earlier uses here as they are found, with dates and links.

## Research principle

Do not force older concepts or other cultures into this framework simply because they resemble it. The goal is comparison and connection, not retrospective ownership.
