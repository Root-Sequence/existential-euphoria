# Existential Euphoria and Root Sequence

**Status:** provisional connection note, in formation. “Good” and “coherent” remain open questions.

> This is what RS is all about. A sort of existential euphoria. A reset of sorts. A realization. Possibility, but actually good (a term to be ‘defined’ TBD). Movement in a ‘coherent’ direction for lack of a better term. Oneness with everything, truly (in a way at least), including the self. Though not to be too grandeuos, which is not required. ## Interpretation for review

The paragraphs and questions below are an AI-assisted interpretation developed in the conversation. They are separate from Rae's original wording and remain open to revision.

Existential euphoria names something Rae recognizes in the orientation behind Root Sequence: a realization of belonging to existence, a renewed sense of possibility, and an interest in helping that possibility become something good. The self is included in that belonging.

A reset here can mean seeing familiar conditions freshly and recognizing that some can change. It does not require erasing history, grief, limits, or what people already depend on. “Oneness” is left open in the original reflection. One possible reading is interdependence while retaining difference, boundaries, and a distinct self; this note does not settle its metaphysical meaning.

The connection can be ordinary. Care, repair, making something understandable, sharing a resource, or noticing a small moment can give it practical expression. Grandeur is optional. Euphoria is not a condition for participating in RS: grief, uncertainty, frustration, and exhaustion also belong.

## What could “actually good” mean?

These are proposed questions, not a completed definition or a universal scoring system:

- Good for whom, over what time, and with whose judgment represented?
- What happens to suffering, dignity, agency, access, and ecological conditions?
- Can affected people refuse, disagree, challenge a decision, and seek repair?
- Who bears the costs, maintenance, dependencies, and unintended consequences?
- What evidence would make us change our interpretation or stop an intervention?

“Coherent” needs similar care. A feeling of unity or an orderly outcome cannot establish that a direction is humane. Differences and disagreement need to remain visible. The Wiki's [False Coherence](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/wiki/blob/main/entities/false-coherence.md) entry provides an existing place to examine that risk.

### Why BHIG matters here

[Being Human(e)](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/beinghumane-guide) describes itself as a practical, evolving field guide grounded in observation and ordinary relationships. Its public scope includes care, harm, boundaries, repair, dependence, dignity, power, grief, and love.

That makes it a useful place to challenge this idea. Feeling connected to everything does not automatically mean acting humanely toward anyone. A practical connection should help someone notice a situation, understand its consequences, or choose a more humane response. It should also leave room for the person's own needs and limits.

[Human(e) Design](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/wiki/blob/main/entities/humane-design.md) and the [Root Sequence Design Grammar](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/wiki/blob/main/entities/root-sequence-design-grammar.md) already provide routes from observations and questions toward project-owned decisions. This note can link to them without replacing their work.

## A small next experiment

Choose one ordinary situation or existing project decision. Describe what becomes possible, who is affected, what could make it good, what could make it harmful, and what would count as learning. Keep the account small enough to inspect and revise.

Possible starting point: a BHIG field note about a moment when feeling connected supported care, or when the language of connection concealed an unmet need or boundary. This is a proposed experiment, not a new assignment or an established result.
